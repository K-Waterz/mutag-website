Business note

A website that looks finished can still be invisible.

A page that opens on your phone is the start, not the job. People have to find it, understand it without effort, and know what to do next. That is the difference between a brochure and a site that can take an enquiry.

A contractor with a Facebook page, a facilities company that lives on referrals, and a professional firm with a handsome site all lose the next job the same way. The person who would have called cannot tell, quickly, what you do and how to reach you. People with the authority to hire you will read a clear page. They will not hunt through a menu, or guess which button is the real one.

Being found

Search is the slow path, and it is the one that keeps working after a campaign ends. The page needs a clear title, a description that matches what you actually sell, and the words your customers use — not only the words on the letterhead. People search the way they speak: the trade, the place, the problem. If the page never uses those words, the site can be well made and still absent when someone goes looking on a Sunday evening.

Referrals still matter, especially in construction and facilities, where the next contract often comes from someone who already watched you work. The site is what that person sends on. If the link opens on a page that does not say, in plain language, what you take on and where you work, the referral dies in the forwarding.

One page, one job

A landing page for one offer should do one job. A company site should make the services easy to tell apart. A firm that does maintenance, projects, and supply should not make a visitor guess which of those they have landed on. Say who it is for, what you do, and what happens if they get in touch. Then stop.

Campaigns fill the gap while search catches up. An email to people who already know you, a post with a real offer, or a paid advert that lands on that one page. If the advert promises a quote and the page tells the company story, the click is wasted. The campaign, the page, and the sentence on the button have to agree.

In this country the enquiry often ends on WhatsApp or in an email, not in a form nobody finishes. That is a normal way to do business. The site’s job is to make that step obvious, and to give the person something specific to reply to, so the first message is not “hi, saw your page”.

After it is live

Keep the site current. A broken form, an old phone number, a price you no longer charge, or a service you stopped offering is a quiet way to lose the work the campaign just paid for. An untouched page also tells a careful buyer something you did not mean to say: that the company might not be minding the details.
